Winternals Software: The Bug That Paid for Itself

HomeHow I LeadHow I Think About TechnologyCareer StoryResumeBusiness ImpactWriting & ProjectsMediumLinkedIn

Technical Support / Software Tester — Nov 1999 to Jan 2000

The Role

Got hired because I knew Windows NT4 cold — the MCP work I’d done wasn’t just exam prep, it was hands-on knowledge I could use in my sleep. Worked alongside Mark Russinovich and Bryce Cogswell testing Sysinternals and Winternals products, and supporting customers with real problems.

What I Did

Found a major bug that had already cost the company over $200K in refunds. The root cause: the install path changed the service load order — one way the software worked, the other it didn’t. The path that broke it happened to be the way the MCSE taught you to install NT. Once I found the root cause, they fixed it and recovered $90K+ in buybacks from customers who needed the product within two weeks of reaching out.

Customer Stories

  • NASA Johnson Space Center — solved an admin’s issue with the software. His response: “I know a group of MCSEs that are going to be out of a job after this.”
  • Monday Night Football — the machine controlling the first down line graphics was locked out right before a game. The admin had been fired and locked the system on his way out, as was typical back then. Got them back in.
